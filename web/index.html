Fourier Knobs

Any sound clip can be rebuilt exactly from a list of sine waves, each with its own loudness and starting point: two knobs per wave. What happens if you keep only some of the knobs?

The idea comes from a llama redrawn from more and more Fourier coefficients. Sound turns out to behave rather differently.

What's going on

Coefficients are sine waves
A clip of length T is treated as one loop of a repeating signal. Coefficient k is a sine wave at k / T Hz lasting the whole clip. The plucked notes are 1 s long, so their coefficients sit 1 Hz apart: 22,051 of them, or 44,102 knobs. A 5 s clip has five times as many, 0.2 Hz apart.
First N is a low-pass filter
Keeping coefficients 0 to N−1 keeps everything below N / T Hz and nothing above. The plucked notes start at 440 Hz, so "First 100" is silence; on a 5 s clip it reaches only 20 Hz. Bass comes back first; brightness last.
Largest N is the llama
Keep the loudest coefficients instead, and the notes come back quickly. But every coefficient lasts the whole clip, so dropping the rest stops them cancelling out where there should be silence. You hear the notes before they're played: pre-echo. Watch the orange waveform fill the first quarter-second.
Phase is where the timing lives
Zero and Random keep every coefficient's loudness and change only where each wave starts. The spectrum doesn't move, the energy doesn't change, and the sound is ruined. Zero phase lines every wave up at the start of the loop: one big click, and the clip becomes a palindrome. Random phase spreads the notes into an even wash with no attacks.
Bits per knob
Each knob is rounded to 2B settings: loudness in even dB steps, phase in even angles. 8 bits is close to perfect; at 2 bits the rounding is so coarse that quiet waves jump to full volume. The kbps figure is what the knob settings would cost to send; a typical MP3 is 128 kbps.
Longer clips
Try 1 s, then 5 s, then 30 s of the same music at the same N. The longer the clip, the more coefficients it takes just to reach the bass, and the further each kept wave smears: pre-echo can arrive seconds early.
Why MP3 doesn't do it this way
Real codecs chop the sound into ~20 ms frames and do this per frame, so a coefficient only has to describe a moment, not the whole song. That fixes the smearing you hear here.
